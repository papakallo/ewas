\subsection{Strategic Approach}  
The proposed solution addresses the critical security vulnerabilities of the Baltic region by fundamentally shifting the paradigm of early warning capabilities from a reactive, ground-based radar posture to a proactive, space-based observation framework. Ground-based systems are inherently limited by the curvature of the Earth, terrain masking, and fixed geographical positioning, which severely restricts their line-of-sight against low-altitude or highly compressed ballistic trajectories. This limitation manifests as the "insufficient warning times" currently challenging Baltic national security authorities. 

To overcome these limitations, the concept relies on a hybrid satellite constellation composed of 6 \abbr{GEO} satellites and 72 \abbr{LEO} satellites deployed at an altitude of approximately 1,200 km and evenly distributed across 8 orbital planes with 9 satellites per plane to ensure continuous global coverage.\footnote{The constellation sizing is justified by orbital mechanics simulations (\abbr{GMAT}) and coverage analysis (\abbr{VTS} Timeloop). The 72 \abbr{LEO} satellites at approximately 1,200 km altitude are distributed across 8 orbital planes (9 satellites each) so that at any given moment the constellation maintains uninterrupted visual coverage of the entire globe and no region falls outside sensor range between successive passes. The 6 \abbr{GEO} satellites are positioned to provide at least two independent geostationary vantage points over the primary area of interest, ensuring boost-phase detection and redundancy against single-satellite outages.} This specific architecture was selected over an alternative \abbr{MEO}/\abbr{LEO} framework based on a rigorous trade study. While \abbr{MEO} layers offer wider coverage outside the equatorial region, operating within the inner Van Allen radiation belt severely compromises sensor lifespans, drives up lifecycle costs and introduces critical detection gaps during initial launch sequences. The chosen \abbr{GEO}/\abbr{LEO} hybrid mitigates these risks. The \abbr{GEO} segment is primarily dedicated to detecting the initial booster phase of a missile launch, when the thermal signature is strongest and earliest detection is possible. The \abbr{LEO} segment then resolves polar coverage limitations and provides follow-on observation, enabling continuous tracking of the missile trajectory and supporting a more accurate preliminary impact-area estimation. 

Rather than waiting for a ballistic missile to clear international borders or rise to a high altitude to be detected by ground radars, this concept focuses entirely on capturing the massive electromagnetic and thermal energy emitted during the initial ignition sequence (the boost phase). By detecting launch events as early as possible and maintaining uninterrupted tracking thereafter, the system is designed to provide national authorities with timely warning information for crisis management, civil protection, and emergency response. In addition, the system includes secure storage of historical event data to support post-event analysis, threat assessment, resilience planning, and information sharing with allied governments and international crisis-response mechanisms. 

\subsection{System Architecture} 
To realize this capability, the system is conceptually divided into three highly integrated, mutually supportive segments: the Space Segment, the Ground Segment, the User and Data Segment. Together, these segments form a fully automated pipeline that converts satellite observations into actionable warning products for national decision-makers. 


\subsection{The Space Segment}
The Space Segment forms the core sensing layer of the system and consists of the proposed 6 \abbr{GEO} satellites and 72 \abbr{LEO} satellites. The \abbr{GEO} satellites provide persistent wide-area observation and are optimized for the earliest detection of missile launches during the boost phase, when the thermal plume is most intense and the launch location can be identified rapidly. As a \abbr{GEO} sensor detects an initial thermal burst, it performs preliminary onboard denoising to filter out solar bursts and terrestrial noise. If classified as a valid missile threat, the \abbr{GEO} satellite automatically transmits the target's precise coordinates to the two closest \abbr{LEO} satellites. Upon receiving these cross-cueing coordinates, these \abbr{LEO} satellites rapidly orient themselves toward the designated location to track the rising missile. By observing the target simultaneously, these two tracking satellites calculate a preliminary trajectory and transmit the encrypted telemetry data down to the Ground Segment. 

This combined constellation ensures continuous surveillance, high temporal coverage, and resilient detection performance across the Baltic region and beyond. The sensors aboard the satellites are designed to detect transient infrared signatures associated with missile launches and to support automated cueing of follow-on observations. 


\subsection{The Ground Segment} 
The Ground Segment provides telemetry reception, mission control, and data processing. It is designed as a resilient and geographically distributed network of secure ground stations and processing facilities. Its primary function is to receive downlinked data from the Space Segment, process it with low latency, and transform raw observations into validated warning outputs. 

Driven by the need for rapid and reliable performance, the ground infrastructure relies on automated processing pipelines and secure communications links. Upon receiving the encrypted telemetry data from the \abbr{LEO} layer, the Ground Station decrypts it and verifies data integrity. Thereafter, the ground architecture utilizes high-speed edge computing to calculate the definitive missile trajectory and projected impact zones while stripping away environmental background noise such as solar glint off the Baltic Sea, cloud reflections, or industrial thermal outputs, leaving a clean, high-fidelity tracking signal. These systems are intended to ensure robust operation under contested or degraded conditions and to minimize the time between detection and dissemination of warning information. 


\subsection{The User and Data Segment} 
The final segment is the functional bridge to civilian and military decision-makers. It operates on a dual-pipeline concept: 

    The Immediate Alerting Pipeline: An automated, deterministic routing system that converts raw positional data into standardized air-defense and civil-protection formats, pushing immediate warning telemetry to national command centers. 

    The Telemetry Analytics Pipeline: A sovereign, highly encrypted data repository that securely stores every raw and processed dataset generated by the system for post-event analysis and strategic long-term planning. 

\subsection{Core Functional Pillars}  
The mission requirements are met through three distinct, conceptually separated functional operations within the system architecture. 

\paragraph{Pillar I: Boost-Phase Infrared Detection}
The system's earliest warning capability is based on infrared detection of the missile's boost phase. The primary technical mechanism for achieving the "earliest possible" detection relies on \abbr{IR} sensing. During launch, ballistic missiles generate a strong thermal plume from burning propellants, producing a distinct infrared signature that can be detected from space. The \abbr{GEO} satellites are primarily responsible for this initial detection function, providing persistent observation while executing preliminary denoising and initiating rapid automated cueing of the tracking layer upon identifying a valid launch event. 

\paragraph{Pillar II: Trajectory Tracking and Impact Estimation}
Once a launch is detected, the \abbr{LEO} constellation takes over the tracking function. The concept utilizes multi-satellite observation. When a launch event is triggered, adjacent satellites in the constellation cross-cue their sensors to observe the missile plume from multiple angles simultaneously. Specifically, the two closest \abbr{LEO} satellites orient toward the cross-cued coordinates, track the object to calculate a preliminary trajectory, and relay encrypted telemetry to the ground. Using multiple observation angles the satellites provide the geometric data needed to estimate the missile's trajectory with increasing confidence.  

\paragraph{Pillar III: Low-Latency Dissemination and Sovereign Archiving}
To maximize warning value, the system is designed for rapid automated dissemination of validated information to national authorities. Warning messages are formatted at the ground station into lightweight, automated protocols that directly feed into national civil defense interfaces. Simultaneously, to address the mission's mandates for resilience planning and information sharing, all relevant data is securely archived to create a historical record of missile activity. This repository acts as a digital black box of regional airspace. Post-event, this data can be replayed to analyze adversary capabilities, refine air defense configurations, and improve national resilience structures.
