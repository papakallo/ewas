The primary use scenario of \abbr{EWAS} is missile threat detection and alert generation and dissemination. The flow of events in this scenario is generally linear and begins with the detection of a thermal change, which might indicate a missile launch, by autonomous satellite constellation. Such event serves as an entry point to a processing pipeline ending with alert dissemination as well as storing the event data for future uses.

\textbf{Primary actors}: Satellite operators, national authorities 

\textbf{Event flow}: 

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Wide-field infrared sensor located aboard a \abbr{GEO} satellite detects a thermal spike and flags the event.

    \item Onboard processor filters out noise and determines the source is a missile.

    \item The two \abbr{LEO} satellites closest to the detected location orient their narrow-field sensors toward the moving target, perform stereoscopic tracking, and calculate a preliminary trajectory estimate.

    \item The two \abbr{LEO} satellites downlink their encrypted telemetry and preliminary trajectory estimate to the ground segment, which verifies the data, calculates the definitive trajectory, and estimates the missile impact zone.

    \item The system operator's console displays a warning and detailed information about the incoming missile and waits for confirmation. 

    \item Information about the missile event is written to a database after the operator's confirmation. 


    \item The system generates and disseminates adequate warnings to the authorities of endangered areas. 
\end{enumerate}
