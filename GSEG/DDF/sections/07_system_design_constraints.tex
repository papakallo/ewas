\section{7. System Design Constraints}\label{system-design-constraints}

\emph{The provisions below are constraints imposed by the segment
design. They do not replace the TRS
requirements or operating procedures.}

\subsection{7.1 Production and Integration
Constraints}\label{production-and-integration-constraints}

The adopted GSEG architecture imposes the following constraints on the
manufacture, installation and integration of its elements.

\hline

\hline

\textbf{DC-P01} & All fixed GSEG infrastructure, including GSN and GIS
equipment, must fit within \textbf{one geographical site}. Antenna
location is constrained by local spacecraft visibility, RF conditions
and permitted installation conditions. \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-P02} & The shared GIS platform must permit deployment of
critical C\&C, DPS and EWS services across \textbf{two separate
equipment failure domains} on the site. Components of redundant critical
paths must not depend on the same single host. \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-P03} & Critical warning-path resources must be separable from
MCC and DAS workloads. Integration of these services on a common
platform must not create a dependency that blocks the first warning. \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-P04} & The interface architecture must permit
\textbf{isolated connection of an SSEG emulator and a test recipient}.
Test commands and warnings must not reach operational interfaces through
a configuration intended for testing. \\*
\end{longtable}
\endgroup

The assembly sequence, commissioning schedule and test procedures are
not specified at this level. They belong in the integration and
verification plans.

\hline

\textbf{DC-O01} & The first warning must not depend on a contemporaneous
MCC operator decision, completion of a DAS write or availability of the
operator interface (\texttt{GSEG-\allowbreak{}REQ-\allowbreak{}ARC-\allowbreak{}002}). \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-O02} & Shared GIS infrastructure must protect
critical-service execution time from non-critical workloads. The
\textbf{2 s and 15 s} allocations also include internal transport and
failover delays; no additional time outside these limits is foreseen. \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-O03} & Automatic selection of two LEO spacecraft by C\&C must
not require an ad hoc orbit calculation or approval by an MCC operator.
The design requires previously prepared, traceable orbital products and
a mechanism for validating their currency. Validity conditions will be
defined in the MCC--C\&C interface. \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-O04} & For the preliminary sizing case of \textbf{10
simultaneous events} and a single failure covered by redundancy, each of
the two GIS domains is designed to take over the entire critical-service
workload. This is a \textbf{proposed design allocation (P)} from Chapter
6, not a demonstrated capability. \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-O05} & The design of critical services must permit readiness
monitoring at the GSN and EWS boundaries. Unavailability during planned
maintenance, a fault or recovery that prevents the performance of
critical functions is counted in assessment of the required
≥99.99\% availability. \\*
\end{longtable}
\endgroup

These constraints do not establish resilience to complete loss of the
site or external links. The consequences of such failures have not yet
been resolved.

\subsection{7.3 Transportation and Storage
Constraints}\label{transportation-and-storage-constraints}

The following constraints concern \textbf{physical GSEG equipment and
delivery packages}, not the five-year mission-data retention performed
by DAS.

\hline
\endhead
\hline
\endlastfoot
\textbf{DC-T01} & Transport and storage of antenna, RF, computing,
mass-storage, power and cooling equipment are constrained by
manufacturer-permitted conditions, especially temperature, humidity,
shock, vibration and electrostatic-discharge protection. No numerical
values are prescribed before equipment selection. \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-T02} & Packaging and movement of elements requiring alignment
or calibration must not prevent their inspection and re-alignment after
installation. \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-T03} & Transferred media, configurations, keys and software
packages must preserve integrity, version traceability and protection
against unauthorized access during transport and storage. \\*
\end{longtable}
\endgroup

\textbf{DC-M01} & The two GIS failure domains must permit independent
isolation of one domain for servicing without requiring the other to be
shut down. Maintaining critical services in this state is constrained by
the workload allocated to the surviving domain (Chapter 6, status P). \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-M02} & Equipment and links requiring maintenance must remain
accessible for diagnosis, replacement or restoration without
compromising the physical separation of the two GIS domains. Shared site
utilities and external links are not assumed to be serviceable without
mission impact. \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-M03} & Critical-service configurations must permit
unambiguous identification of the deployed version and restoration of an
earlier compatible configuration. The design must allow changes to be
checked without sending test commands to the operational constellation
or test warnings to actual recipients. \\ \hline
\textbf{DC-M04} & GIS and mission-service diagnostic interfaces must
permit recognition of loss of a required capability and identification
of the unavailable resource or connection for MCC supervision
(\texttt{GSEG-\allowbreak{}REQ-\allowbreak{}REL-\allowbreak{}002}). \\*
\end{longtable}
\endgroup

Detailed repair times, spare parts, maintenance tools and failover
procedures will be defined at lower levels. Demonstration of service
continuity during maintenance and analysis of common-cause failures
belong in the DDJF and verification documentation.
